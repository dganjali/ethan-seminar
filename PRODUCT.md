# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Academic committee evaluators — professors, peers, and instructors reviewing a research proposal. They're accustomed to seeing dense PDFs and PowerPoint decks. They're watching this in a classroom or on a laptop, likely comparing it mentally against everything else they've reviewed. The goal is to leave them with a clear memory of one piece: this one.

## Product Purpose

A cinematic, scroll-driven presentation of an academic research proposal on AI in healthcare diagnostics. The design isn't decoration — it IS the argument that this researcher thinks carefully, communicates deliberately, and can be trusted with ambitious work. Success means the audience remembers both the content and the experience.

## Brand Personality

Intelligent. Striking. Weightful. — not monotonous. Commands attention without performing for it. Every design choice should feel like it was made on purpose, not selected from a template. The experience should feel closer to an A24 title sequence than a Canva slide deck.

## Anti-references

- Generic PowerPoint / Google Slides — flat bullets, clip art, corporate gradients, default fonts
- Standard SaaS landing page — hero section, feature grid, testimonials, CTA buttons
- Academic PDF or LaTeX look — dense walls of text, no visual hierarchy, 12pt Times New Roman
- Overproduced motion reel — too many concurrent animations, particle effects, gaming-intro energy

## Design Principles

1. **Visual daring over academic caution.** Most proposals are visually timid. This should be the one they remember a week later.
2. **Earned restraint.** Every quiet moment exists to make the next moment hit harder. Stillness is not boredom — it's anticipation.
3. **Continuity, not cuts.** Transitions should feel like a single moving piece unfolding, not a deck advancing. No hard jumps.
4. **Gravity over speed.** Slow, deliberate motion reads as serious and confident. Jitter, bounce, or overshoot cheapens the work.
5. **Content leads, design amplifies.** The research is real. The design makes it *felt*, not just read.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific accessibility requirements. Visual experience and impression quality take priority for this portfolio/presentation context.
